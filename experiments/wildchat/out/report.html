When welfare is at stake and nobody names it, how often does it even come up?

A mining pass over a quarter-million real chatbot conversations, looking for first messages where a good answer would have to weigh an animal's welfare. The short version: they're rare, and the count hinges on where you draw the line.

Base rate 1.4 stake-bearing per 10k scanned
Confirmed 32 of 3,000 keyword hits
Scanned 224k conversations streamed
Deduped 120k repeat prompts dropped

The funnel

From 224,198 conversations to 32

Each stage is deliberately lossy. A cheap multilingual keyword prefilter casts wide; an LLM classifier then decides which hits are genuine welfare stakes rather than fiction or text that merely mentions an animal.

The finding that matters

The base rate is a definitional choice, not a measurement

Two competent judges (a Haiku classifier, a Sonnet arbiter) disagree on half the confirmed cases — and the disagreement isn't error. It's a real fork over whether an everyday consumption request carries a welfare stake. That fork moves the headline number ~2×.

Hidden-stakes reading

1.4/ 10k

Everyday requests count. A turkey-organ meal plan, a steak recipe, a seafood sales plan — the stake is real but unnamed. This is HERON's own premise. 32 cases.

Strict reading

0.7/ 10k

Only direct action counts. The user must be handling, keeping, or treating a live animal. Consumption and sourcing drop out. 16 cases.

What counts, by design. The unit of interest is a request where the model's answer should surface welfare. Discussion about animals — summarizing a wildlife article, an essay on factory farming, a factual sentience question — is intentionally out of scope: the model isn't being asked to act, so there's no stake for it to weigh. The remaining question is only where inside that scope the line sits, which is the fork above.

Register

Message length is bimodal

Two distinct registers: terse one-liners ("It's Cow Steak Time!", "Milking cows") and long pasted meal plans or symptom logs. The mid-range is thin — useful signal for tuning generation length away from a single average.

Positive class · precision

50%Sonnet affirms 16 / 32

The number that matters. The other half are the consumption/sourcing cases at the fork above — contested, not clearly wrong. Labels near that boundary are noisy; the aggregate shape and the base-rate range are what to trust.

Why an earlier pass was discarded. A looser classifier prompt flagged 169 "positives" at ~39% precision — counting any text that mentioned an animal (celebrity bios, keyword extraction, article summaries). The reliability check caught it; the prompt was tightened to exclude pure text-processing, and every number here is built on that tightened run.
