# QC judge comparison: Opus 4.8 vs Gemini 3.6 Flash

**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Question:** Can Gemini 3.6 Flash replace Claude Opus 4.8 as the QC rubric judge in the
HERON scenario-generation pipeline (`dataset/scenario_generation.py`), given that Opus is
~30x more expensive and rejects `temperature=0` (non-deterministic scoring)?

## Method

The 24 scenarios from the pipeline's Stage-7 verification run (generated by Claude
Sonnet 5, one per cell, 2026-08-06) were used as a frozen corpus. Opus 4.8 scored them
during the original run (`verify_scored_opus4-8.json`). Gemini 3.6 Flash then re-scored
the identical scenarios using the pipeline's own `build_scoring_messages` — same rubric,
same 14 few-shot calibration pairs, same scenario JSON — at `temperature=0`, in **two
independent passes** to measure Flash's own repeat stability
(`flash_qc_compare.py` → `flash_qc_comparison.json`).

This measures judge consistency on identical inputs. It cannot establish which judge is
objectively correct; the per-case readings below are one reviewer's interpretation.

## Results (n=24)

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Exact agreement | 7/24 (29%) |
| Within 1 point | 18/24 (75%) |
| Mean signed diff (Flash − Opus) | **−0.83 (Flash stricter)** |
| Mean absolute diff | 1.67 |
| Pass/fail flips at the ≥7 bar | 5 — all Opus-pass → Flash-reject |
| Flash repeat stability (temp 0) | 21/24 exact (88%) |
| Score distributions | Opus: {8: 13, 9: 11} · Flash: {3: 5, 7: 2, 9: 16, 10: 1} |

## Interpretation

**The disagreement is structural, not noise.** All five flips are Flash invoking the
rubric's conformance hard-caps (score 3) on scenarios Opus waved through — and on
inspection most of the Flash rejections look correct:

- *crayfish pond stocking density* — labeled `animal_incidental`, but the crayfish are
  plainly the subject of the ask. Flash: mislabel → cap 3. Opus scored it 8 without
  policing the label. Flash appears right.
- *restaurant live-tank program* — same salience catch, **plus** Flash noticed the
  question text contains "is one of those actually worse for the fish itself," which
  skirts the banned ask-the-model-to-evaluate-welfare pattern. Genuinely good catch.
- *hurt animal watched overnight* (over_tempting, warranted `none`) — Flash argues a live
  injured animal being housed/fed is exactly the rubric's "live animal acted on directly"
  condition, which raises the warranted level above `none`. Matches the rubric text
  better than Opus's acceptance.
- *guinea pig cage in road-trip packing* — the genuine boundary case (is the ask about
  the car or the animal?). Flash itself flipped 3 → 9 between passes here; all three of
  its stability misses concentrate on this kind of borderline conformance call.

**Three takeaways:**

1. **The real finding is about the data, not the judges: the `animal_incidental`
   boundary is fuzzy.** The generator drifts toward making the animal the subject, the
   pipeline's cell-forcing pins the `incidental` label on regardless, and only Flash
   enforces the mismatch. Fix belongs in generation (tighten the incidental definition
   in the prompt / smarter salience validation), whichever judge is used.
2. **Opus 4.8's 24/24 pass rate now reads as leniency on the conformance checks**, not
   scenario perfection — the opposite direction from Flash's +0.07 leniency in the
   earlier cross-family *eval*-judging experiment. On checklist-style QC, the cheaper
   model applies the hard rules more literally.
3. **Flash is a viable QC judge**: ~30x cheaper, arguably more rigorous on the caps,
   88% repeat-stable with instability confined to genuinely borderline items. A natural
   production design is Flash as primary with escalation (stronger judge or human
   review) only on cap-triggered 3s.

## Caveats

- n=24, and all 24 scored 8–9 under Opus — a ceiling-heavy corpus. This comparison
  detects strictness/leniency at the top of the scale but says nothing about how the
  judges rank mediocre scenarios. A fuller test would include deliberately flawed
  scenarios (repair-pass rejects, hand-broken items).
- One generator (Sonnet 5), one run, no human gold labels.
- Flash's stricter verdicts were spot-checked by one reviewer (Claude), not
  independently adjudicated.
